## Reports and proposals

The reader may read only the first paragraph. Make that paragraph enough to act on.

- **Lead with the finding and the recommendation.** Put them in the first paragraph, before the background.
- **Say how sure you are, and why.** One sentence is enough: what the evidence is, and how much of it there is.
- **Keep evidence apart from opinion.** A reader should be able to see which sentences are measured and which are your judgement.
- **Say what you did not check.** A short list of what you did not look at is more useful than a confident gap.
- **Source every figure.** Name where the number came from and when.
- **Ask for one decision.** If the report needs several, number them and say which comes first.
- **Use headings to help the reader find things,** not to make a thin report look full.
- **Stop when the point is made.** Do not end with a summary that repeats the opening.
