Returns kiosk pilot: six-week update

As many of you will know, over the past period we have been trialling a self-service returns kiosk, and this paper sets out where things have got to with a view to informing next steps going forward.

The pilot ran for six weeks at two stores, Hexham and Morpeth. At Hexham, the average time to handle a return fell from 7 minutes to 4 minutes. We do not have a reliable figure for Morpeth, because its kiosk was offline for 9 of the 42 days.

We surveyed customers who used the kiosk. There were 62 responses, and 71% rated it good or very good. This is a small sample, and we only asked people who used the kiosk, not those who queued at the till instead.

There is a disagreement about staff time. Gareth, the Morpeth store manager, says the time staff saved was mostly spent helping customers use the kiosk. The regional lead thinks this will settle down once customers are used to it. We have not resolved this.

Hexham also had two suspected fraudulent returns during the pilot. We do not know whether either was linked to the kiosk.

My recommendation is to extend the pilot to four more stores for three months, not to roll it out to all 40 stores yet.

Each kiosk costs £3,100, plus about £150 a month for support. The supplier's quote is valid until 30th November.

We did not look at weekend trading or at the effect on staff morale.

I need a decision from the operations director by 21st November, so that the order can be placed before the quote runs out.
