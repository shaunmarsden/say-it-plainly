Returns kiosk pilot: six-week update

I recommend extending the pilot to four more stores for three months, not rolling it out to all 40 stores yet. I need a decision from the operations director by 21st November, so that the order can be placed before the supplier's quote runs out on 30th November. The evidence is limited: only one of the two pilot stores gave a reliable time figure, and the survey covered 62 kiosk users.

What we found

The pilot ran for six weeks at two stores, Hexham and Morpeth. At Hexham, the average time to handle a return fell from 7 minutes to 4 minutes. We do not have a reliable figure for Morpeth, because its kiosk was offline for 9 of the 42 days.

We surveyed customers who used the kiosk. There were 62 responses, and 71% rated it good or very good. This is a small sample, and we only asked people who used the kiosk, not those who queued at the till instead.

What we do not know

Staff time: Gareth, the Morpeth store manager, says the time staff saved was mostly spent helping customers use the kiosk. The regional lead thinks this will settle down once customers are used to it. We have not resolved this.

Fraud: Hexham had two suspected fraudulent returns during the pilot. We do not know whether either was linked to the kiosk.

What we did not look at: weekend trading, and the effect on staff morale.

Cost

Each kiosk costs £3,100, plus about £150 a month for support. The supplier's quote is valid until 30th November.

Decision needed

Please decide by 21st November whether to extend the pilot to four more stores for three months.
