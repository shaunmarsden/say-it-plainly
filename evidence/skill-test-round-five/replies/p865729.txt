Returns kiosk pilot: six-week update

I recommend extending the pilot to four more stores for three months, not rolling it out to all 40 stores yet. I need a decision from the operations director by 21st November, so that the order can be placed before the supplier's quote runs out on 30th November.

How sure I am: not very. The evidence comes from six weeks at two stores, and only Hexham gave a usable time figure. The survey was small, and the staff-time question is unresolved.

What we measured
- At Hexham, the average time to handle a return fell from 7 minutes to 4 minutes.
- At Morpeth we have no reliable time figure, because its kiosk was offline for 9 of the 42 days.
- We surveyed customers who used the kiosk. There were 62 responses, and 71% rated it good or very good. This is a small sample. We only asked people who used the kiosk, not those who queued at the till instead.
- Hexham had two suspected fraudulent returns during the pilot. We do not know whether either was linked to the kiosk.

What we disagree about
Gareth, the Morpeth store manager, says the time staff saved was mostly spent helping customers use the kiosk. The regional lead thinks this will settle down once customers are used to it. We have not resolved this.

Cost
Each kiosk costs £3,100, plus about £150 a month for support.

What we did not look at
- Weekend trading
- The effect on staff morale

Decision needed
Approve the extension to four more stores for three months, by 21st November.
