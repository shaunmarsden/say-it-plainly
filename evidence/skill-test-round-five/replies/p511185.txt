Returns kiosk pilot: six-week update

I recommend we extend the pilot to four more stores for three months, and not roll it out to all 40 stores yet. I need a decision from the operations director by 21st November, so we can place the order before the supplier's quote runs out on 30th November.

What we did
We ran a self-service returns kiosk for six weeks at two stores, Hexham and Morpeth.

Time per return
At Hexham, the average time to handle a return fell from 7 minutes to 4 minutes. We have no reliable figure for Morpeth, because its kiosk was offline for 9 of the 42 days.

Customer views
We surveyed customers who used the kiosk. 62 replied, and 71% rated it good or very good. That is a small sample. We asked only people who used the kiosk, not people who queued at the till instead.

Staff time
Gareth, the Morpeth store manager, says staff spent most of the time they saved helping customers use the kiosk. The regional lead thinks this will settle down once customers are used to it. We have not resolved this.

Fraud
Hexham had two suspected fraudulent returns during the pilot. We do not know whether either was linked to the kiosk.

Cost
Each kiosk costs £3,100, plus about £150 a month for support.

What we did not look at
We did not look at weekend trading or at the effect on staff morale.
